# SNHU-Travel-Sprint-Review-and-Retrospective

For this course, I included my Sprint Review and Retrospective from the SNHU Travel project. Throughout this project, I explored how Agile and Scrum practices can be used to organize a development team, respond to changing requirements, and create software that focuses on the needs of the user.

# How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user wants to accomplish and then translating those needs into specific features that can be developed and tested. User stories help with this process because they describe requirements from the user's perspective instead of focusing only on technical details. This allows developers to understand not only what needs to be created, but also why the feature is important to the user.

User stories can then be divided into smaller development tasks and given acceptance criteria that clearly define what must be accomplished for the story to be considered complete. Throughout the SNHU Travel project, this helped keep development focused on customer needs while also giving the team clear and manageable goals for each sprint.

# How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by first understanding the requirements and then breaking larger problems into smaller, manageable tasks. Instead of attempting to develop an entire application at once, I prefer an iterative approach where individual features can be developed, tested, reviewed, and improved over time.

In my future development work, I hope to incorporate several Agile processes, including user stories, Product Backlogs, Sprint Backlogs, Daily Scrum meetings, Sprint Reviews, and Sprint Retrospectives. I especially value the flexibility that Agile provides. Requirements can change during development, and an Agile approach allows the team to reprioritize work without having to restart the entire project. Sprint Reviews and Retrospectives are also valuable because they provide opportunities to receive feedback and identify ways to improve both the product and the development process.

# What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, collaborating with others, and being transparent about progress and obstacles. Software development requires contributions from people with different responsibilities, so everyone needs to understand the team's goals and be willing to support one another.

A good team member should also be adaptable and open to feedback. If requirements change or another team member identifies a problem, the goal should be to work together to find a solution rather than remain committed to an approach that is no longer effective. The SNHU Travel project demonstrated how important communication is within an Agile environment. Practices such as Daily Scrum meetings allow team members to discuss progress and challenges while keeping everyone focused on the sprint goal. Ultimately, being dependable, communicative, adaptable, and willing to collaborate helps the entire development team produce a stronger product.
